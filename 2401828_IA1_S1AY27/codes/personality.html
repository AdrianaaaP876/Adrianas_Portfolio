MY TOP 3 TRAITS

Trait 01

Creative

I enjoy finding new ways to approach problems, whether that's designing a project, writing a story, or coming up with a memorable way to study. My creativity keeps schoolwork interesting and helps my assignments stand out.

Trait 02

Resilient

When I get a bad grade or face a tough subject, I don't give up. I figure out what went wrong and try again, determined to do better on the next. This helps me stay calm during exams and keep improving.

Trait 03

Curious

I like asking "why" and "how" and learning beyond what's in the textbook. My curiosity makes me research and learn things on my own, which deepens my understanding and further my skills.

LLMs: A TOOL OR A CRUTCH?

Posted by: Adriana Pinnock | Date:Thursday, October 1, 2026

Introduction

A crutch carries your weight for you, so you wouldn't have to, whereas a tool simply multiplies the effort you are already putting in. To me? That is the whole meaning of the statemnt: “LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch”. The question isn't whether or not it's helpful to us students, it's asking if after using it, can I do more on my own, or less? We can see calculators as tools becuase we still understand the math it's doing for us. Think about it, you'd have to know the formula to put it in the calculator, this shows you have understanding of what you're doing and can let the calculator do the work. In other words, you could do it yourself, but the calculator allows you to get to the answer faster, without replacing the understanding that led you to the formula in the first place. When using LLMs, who's doing the thinking, you or it?

Let's take a step further a understand what are LLMs. These are Large Language Models, it's a type of AI system trained on massive amounts of data in order to generate and translate text and other content. One thing to note is that what it generates is not always necessarily correct. It may sound right, but it isnt fact checked. How can you be sure your answer not based off outdated data? LLMs are fast,yes, and it can help get things done quickly, but it may not always be accurate, it can simply be confidently incorrect. This is why the generated responses of LLMs should not be taken at face value. Do your own research , check your answer against trusted sources, and make sure you understand what it's saying before using it.

My position on the statement: “LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch”, is simple: LLMs make me a better student when I use it to increase my knowledge, but a worse one when I just use it to "get something out the way".

Evidence to Support

Reseacher Bastani et al. (2025) did an experiment with a students from a high school in Turkey to test how AI and AI tutors have influenced students learning, particularly in mathematics. During the first part, with nearly 1000 students across about 50 math classes, they randomly placed each class in one of three groups: (1) No access to any AI tools, (2) a regular GPT-4 chat interface similar to ChatGPT and (3) a GPT tutor, a specially designed version of GPT-4 with guidelines and restrictions based on teachers' input. In the second part however, all students completed their exams independently. After the experiment commenced, results showed that students who had access to GPT-4 had a 48% improvement, while students who only used GPT Tutors improved by 127%. Though, when access to these were taken away, they performed worse than students who never had access at all.

This experiment shows that LLMs can improve students' performace, but relying on it too much can affect their ability to work independently.

To further explain this point, Gerlich (2025) found that the more people relied on AI the more they offloaded. The offloading he mentioned to refers to cognitive offloading. This is using something outside of your own head to do mental work for you. Gelich explained that both heavier AI use and offloading we strongly linked to weaker critical thinking.

When, Where and How I Use LLMs

My rule is simple: I try it myself first, then I bring in the AI. When I do use it, I ask for questions, hints, feedback or explanations, not finished answers. That way the AI is helping me think instead of thinking for me.

When I'm stuck: Instead of asking for the answer, I ask it to explain the idea in a different way ("explain it like youre talking to a 5 year old"), or to give me an example. Then I reiterate to check that I actually understood it.

Studying for exams: I give it my own notes and ask it to make practice questions or flashcards, then I answer them on my own. Testing myself like this helps me remember far more than just rereading my notes.

After writing a draft: I ask it to point out weak spots in my argument or to argue against my point. The feedback is on my work, and I do the fixing myself.

Starting research: It's useful for brainstorming ideas and search terms I wouldn't have thought of. But I find and read the actual sources myself, because, like I said earlier, LLMs can be confidently incorrect.

It mostly comes down to how I word my prompts. "Solve question 4" uses AI as a crutch. "Here's my attempt at question 4, don't give me the answer, just tell me which step is wrong" uses it as a tool. It's a small change, but it decides who's doing the thinking.

Conclusion

So, should LLMs like ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot be used as a tool and not a crutch? I agree, but I'd add that the AI itself isn't the problem, the way we use it is. So every time I open an AI tool, I try to ask myself the same question from the beginning: who's doing the thinking, me or it? As long as the answer is me, it's a tool.
