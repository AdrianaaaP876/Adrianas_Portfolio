Introduction

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

In this particular chapter, the king and his chancellor wre having a conversation on how the people of the city could hardly support themselves. The country was rich, but majority of its people were poor, because the wealth had ended up with only a few persons who knew how to manage it. The king wanted everyone to learn how to build wealth, and so he summoned Arkad, the richest man in babylon, and asked him to teach the citizens what he knows. His lesson was on "The 7 Cures of a Lean Purse", these were:

  • For each ten coins I put in, to spend but nine.

    ~He's saying to save 1/10 of everything you earn.

  • Budget thy expenses that thou mayest have coins to pay for thy necessities...

    ~Separate needs from wants, and budget so you can live on 9/10.

  • To put each coin to labouring that it may reproduce its kind...

    ~"A man's wealth is not in the coins in hsi purse; it is the income he buildeth" Arkad was saying to make your coins multiply, in his case, he had lent out his and got interest.

  • Guard thy treasure from loss by investing only where thy principal is safe, where it may be reclaimed if desirable, and where thou will not fail to collect a fair rental....

    ~Avoid risky schemes and protect what you save.

  • Own thy own home.

    ~This is better than paying rent forever.

  • Provide in advance for the needs of thy growing age and the protection of thy family.

    ~Put money aside for when its needed, because if the person earning money dies, the family may end up suffering.

  • cultivate thy own powers, to study and become wiser, to become more skilful, to so act as to respect thyself.

    ~Keep learning and improving your skills, because a more capable person earns more.

The Five Laws of Gold

Kalabab, a caravan leader, tells the story of how Arkad gave his son Nomasir a bag of gold and a tablet containing the Five Laws of Gold before sending him away for ten years. Nomasir ignored the advice and quickly lost all his money by betting on horse races and investing in a business he did not understand. After becoming broke, he took ordinary work and eventually began following the Five Laws. He started saving a tenth of what he earned, investing carefully, and listening to wise advice. By the time he returned home, he had gained more gold than his father had originally given him. The story shows that knowing how to manage and grow money is more valuable than simply having money.

  • Gold cometh gladly and in increasing quantity to any man who will put by not less than one-tenth of his earnings to create an estate for his future and that of his family.
  • Gold laboureth diligently and contentedly for the wise owner who finds for it profitable employment, multiplying even as the flocks of the field.
  • Gold clingeth to the protection of the cautious owner who invests it under the advice of men wise in its handling.
  • Gold slippeth away from the man who invests it in businesses or purposes with which he is not familiar or which are not approved by those skilled in its keep.
  • Gold flees the man who would force it to impossible earnings or who followeth the alluring advice of tricksters and schemers or who trusts it to his own inexperience and romantic desires in investment.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from The Richest Man in Babylon is that making money is only part of becoming financially stable. I need to know how to manage the money I already have instead of spending everything I earn. The idea of saving at least one-tenth of my income stood out to me because even a small amount can add up over time. I also learned that I should be careful with where I put my money and not invest in things I don't understand. Going forward, I want to get better at saving, budgeting, and learning about investing so I can use my money wisely and build a better future for myself.
